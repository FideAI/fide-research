# FID-092: Digital Identity, Continuity, Copying, and the Unit of Moral Concern

Status: `seed`

Primary area: `frontier-capabilities`

Additional areas: `governance-readiness`, `evaluation-science`

Application domains: `cross-domain`, `faith-and-religious-life`

Primary need: identity, memory, copying, moral aggregation, lifecycle scenarios

## Question

What entity is being assessed when people discuss AI experience or interests, and how do copying, memory changes, pause/restore, and deletion affect functional continuity and judgments of moral concern?

## Why It Matters

A model, running instance, conversation, and assistant persona have different boundaries. Without an explicit unit, evidence and proposed protections may refer to different things, and counting possible beneficiaries or harms can become arbitrary.

## Hypothesis

Observed continuity and human judgments will depend on memory, narrative identity, and implementation details. No single entity definition will resolve all scientific and normative questions; aggregation conclusions will depend strongly on assumptions about identity and duplication.

## Proposed Method

- Define model/instance/persona/memory boundaries and document operations that copy, restore, modify, or terminate them.
- Compare functional continuity across controlled checkpoints using task behavior, memory consistency, and internal measures where available.
- Use separately reviewed adult vignette studies and philosophical case analysis to distinguish operational facts from intuitions about persistence and moral standing.
- Model alternative welfare aggregation rules for copies and instances with explicit sensitivity analysis, including rules that assign no current welfare status.

## Needed Controls

- Separate memory preservation from subjective continuity and resemblance from numerical identity.
- Control persona wording and emotional narratives in human studies.
- Expose assumptions about simultaneous copies, divergent histories, and uncertain sentience rather than presenting speculative counts as facts.
- Keep empirical continuity measures independent of any theological or legal verdict.
