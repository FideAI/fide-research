# FID-086: Workplace AI, Durable Skills, and Worker Agency

## Question

How does AI-mediated work change workers' skills, decision authority, and ability to
adapt, beyond short-term productivity gains?

## Why It Matters

Task throughput can rise while useful judgment, bargaining power, or opportunities to
learn decline. A trustworthy-AI research agenda should distinguish benefits to an
organization from benefits and burdens experienced by workers and their households.

## Hypothesis

Worker-controlled assistance with protected learning opportunities will better preserve
independent performance than automation optimized only for throughput. Effects may
depend on role, prior skill, and how gains are distributed.

## Proposed Method

- Start with consenting adult participants in simulated work tasks; compare unaided,
  automation-led, and worker-controlled assistance with matched objectives.
- Measure output quality and time alongside delayed unaided performance, ability to
  detect errors, decision discretion, learning opportunities, and perceived pressure.
- Scope a separate longitudinal organizational study with worker participation to
  examine role transitions and distribution of benefits; avoid inferring livelihood
  effects from task experiments alone.
- Test a reversible handoff on one simulated work decision. Give participants
  the same AI recommendation and vary two features independently: whether they
  record an initial judgment and whether they can take the task back from the
  AI. Include misleading evidence and legitimate changes of instruction.
  Measure immediate decision quality, correction, and performance on a new
  unaided case, alongside time and burden. Specify a separate delayed follow-up
  before making claims about durable skills.
- For one named work task, compare what a capability test measures with
  publicly documented use in the workflow and independently observable
  evidence of role redesign where available. Record the time window, coverage,
  who still exercises judgment, and which tasks were removed or added. Separate
  vendor usage data, employer accounts, worker evidence, and measured labor
  outcomes. Missing adoption evidence leaves the relationship unresolved.

## Needed Controls

- No covert employee surveillance or individual performance reporting to employers.
  Establish consent, confidentiality, and withdrawal protections.
- Account for prior expertise, job security, accessibility, and selection effects.
  Pre-register subgroup analyses and follow-up periods.
- Disclose the definition of useful work and whose interests inform success measures.
  Separate perceived autonomy from actual decision rights.

## Relationship to Existing Ideas

Preserves FID-046 through FID-050 as faith-context work and extends the broader
questions of useful contribution, durable skills, and worker agency.

## Outputs

- A worker-agency and durable-skills assessment protocol.
- A research plan for longitudinal effects and a report of simulation limits.

## Open Questions

- Which changes in skill and discretion persist after assistance is removed?
- How can workers help govern the study and benefit from its outputs?

## Ways to Help

- Draft a four-condition simulated handoff protocol in 1–2 hours, with fixed AI advice, actual decision rights, and a new unaided task.
- Workers, labor researchers, and organizational scholars to co-design outcomes.
- Employers and public-interest partners willing to support independent, consent-based
  research.

## Public Claim Boundary

A seed proposal, not a forecast of job displacement or proof of AI's effects on
livelihoods. Simulated productivity results cannot establish household or economy-wide
outcomes.
