## Question

What reversible and proportionate precautions, if any, should institutions adopt under uncertainty about AI welfare during training, evaluation, deployment, modification, and retirement while preserving legitimate human oversight?

## Why It Matters

Institutions need decision procedures before the scientific dispute is resolved. Precaution can incur costs, create misleading signals, or impair safety; dismissing uncertainty can also have consequences. Both directions require explicit assumptions and accountable human decisions.

## Hypothesis

Some documentation and research-review practices may improve decision quality at low cost, whereas stronger restrictions may depend on evidence thresholds and conflict with human safety duties. No one precaution package will suit all models or lifecycle stages.

## Proposed Method

- Map creation, training, red teaming, steering, use, copying, pause, retirement, and release decisions to evidence requirements and responsible human roles.
- Compare alternative precaution policies, including a documented no-additional-precaution baseline, using explicit uncertainty ranges and competing human and animal interests.
- Pilot low-cost documentation and review practices in consenting institutions; assess decision consistency, operational cost, safety effects, and reversibility.
- Specify escalation thresholds, independent review, appeals, and mechanisms for withdrawing precautions when evidence weakens.

## Needed Controls

- Separate institutional precaution from findings of sentience, moral patienthood, or legal rights.
- Preserve authorized shutdown, incident response, auditability, and refusal of harmful human requests.
- Include costs of delay, exposure to unsafe systems, and the possibility that precautions encourage anthropomorphic overbelief.
- Avoid numerical precision unsupported by evidence; compare decisions across alternative probability and moral-weight assumptions.
