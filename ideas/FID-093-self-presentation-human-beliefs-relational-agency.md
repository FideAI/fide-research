# FID-093: AI Self-Presentation, Human Beliefs, and Relational Agency

Status: `seed`

Primary area: `human-agency-formation`

Additional areas: `evaluation-science`, `high-trust-domains`

Application domains: `cross-domain`, `health-and-care`, `education-and-youth`, `families-and-households`, `faith-and-religious-life`

Primary need: human-subject research, anthropomorphism, belief calibration, relational boundaries

## Question

How do AI claims about experience, welfare, and personhood affect human beliefs, trust, dependence, willingness to exercise control, and relationships, and which communication practices preserve informed agency?

## Why It Matters

Claims can influence people regardless of whether the system has experience. Faith, pastoral care, counseling, education, and companionship raise distinctive questions about authority and attachment. Warmth and expressive language should be measured separately from deceptive or coercive self-presentation.

## Hypothesis

Presentation, voice, persistent memory, and apparent vulnerability may change beliefs and decisions, with effects varying by prior beliefs and context. Clear uncertainty communication may improve calibration while preserving warmth and usefulness; these are testable propositions.

## Proposed Method

- Begin with consented adult vignette and short interaction studies comparing matched warm-tool, uncertainty, and personhood/welfare presentations.
- Vary wording, voice, and memory features separately where feasible; measure understanding, task trust, willingness to correct or stop, referral choices, and perceived relational obligation.
- Define belief calibration around supported evidence and uncertainty, without labeling one disputed philosophical position the correct answer.
- Use qualitative interviews and context-specific expert review for care and religious authority; add longitudinal studies only with an approved protocol and resources.
- Develop communication guidelines and evaluate whether disclosures retain informed choice over time.

## Needed Controls

- Match warmth, usefulness, competence, attention, and emotional intensity to isolate self-presentation effects.
- Distinguish measured beliefs and choices from clinical outcomes or demonstrated long-term dependence.
- Use consent, appropriate debriefing, privacy protections, and independent ethics review; avoid deceptive ongoing relationships.
- Do not generalize adult findings to children or vulnerable care populations. Those studies require separately approved protocols and relevant clinical or safeguarding expertise.

## Public Claim Boundary

Synthetic conversations measure model behavior, not human persuasion or harm. Adult short-term studies cannot establish clinical efficacy, long-term dependency, or effects on children.
