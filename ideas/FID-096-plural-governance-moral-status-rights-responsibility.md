# FID-096: Plural Governance of AI Moral Status, Rights, and Human Responsibility

Status: `seed`

Primary area: `governance-readiness`

Additional areas: `human-agency-formation`, `faith-religious-life`

Application domains: `cross-domain`, `public-sector-and-civic-life`, `legal-and-justice`, `faith-and-religious-life`

Primary need: plural deliberation, rights claims, legal scholarship, institutional accountability

## Question

How should societies and institutions make accountable decisions about contested AI moral-status and rights claims while preserving human responsibility and representing scientific, philosophical, religious, and public disagreement?

## Why It Matters

Scientific evidence cannot alone select a moral theory or confer legal status. Conversely, public disagreement does not remove the need for operational decisions. Institutions need transparent procedures and a way to revise them as evidence changes.

## Hypothesis

Structured deliberation can identify practical agreements despite persistent disagreement about consciousness or rights. Separating moral status, legal instruments, institutional duties, and human accountability will clarify policy choices and prevent misplaced authority.

## Proposed Method

- Map arguments for and against different forms of moral consideration, legal recognition, and precaution, distinguishing their evidence and normative premises.
- Use jurisdiction-specific legal scholarship with dated primary sources; separately compare proposed reforms without treating them as current law.
- Pilot facilitated adult deliberation using balanced cases, evidence-depth labels, religious disagreement maps from FID-073, and explicit human-safety tradeoffs.
- Compare institutional arrangements for oversight, public participation, independent assessment, review, and accountability; measure procedural understanding and decision transparency.
- Publish agreements, unresolved disagreements, minority positions, and conditions under which participants would revise a conclusion.

## Needed Controls

- Distinguish moral patienthood, moral agency, legal personhood, spiritual status, and legal rights; none implies the others automatically.
- Do not collapse religious traditions or let one company, funder, or advocacy constituency define the evidence threshold.
- Record recruitment, framing, facilitation, jurisdiction, and expertise limits; deliberative agreement is not proof of a philosophical claim.
- Keep responsibility for deployment and harms with identifiable human institutions in proposed arrangements; examine rather than assume liability consequences.
