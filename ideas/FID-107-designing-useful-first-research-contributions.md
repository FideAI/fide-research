## Question

Which task designs help first-time research contributors produce a result
another researcher can use within an agreed time and review budget?

## Why It Matters

An open problem can be important yet require unadvertised access, expertise,
or reviewer time. Fide already has a way to propose and coordinate tasks.
A study can test whether a better starting packet closes a real barrier for
contributors and whether maintaining that packet costs less than the rework
it prevents.

## Hypothesis

A scoped task with pinned evidence, a worked example using a different case,
and a clear path for recording blockers will yield more independently usable
first outputs than a well-written task brief alone. Preparation costs or
limited reviewer availability may offset the improvement.

## Proposed Method

- Prepare two small, comparable tasks using the existing research-task template.
  Start with a two-source synthesis under FID-045 or an offline aggregate
  check under FID-106. Give both versions a defined output, eligibility
  criteria, time cap, public starting materials, and equal access to review.
  Add pinned inputs, a small worked example, and a blocker checklist to one
  version. Dry-run the tasks for feasibility before any participant study.
- If a separately reviewed opt-in pilot is feasible, compare the versions
  across matched tasks with comparable prerequisite skills and support. Have
  an independent reviewer score each artifact against predeclared completion
  and evidence criteria, blinded to task version when practical.
- Count usable outputs per attempted task, supported negative or inconclusive
  results, blockers, rework, contributor time, and total preparation and
  reviewer time. Record what the coordinator would reuse and report the
  results at cohort level.

## Needed Controls

- Keep access to support and ordinary participation separate from research
  consent. Use no private contributor records, unsolicited invitations, or
  public individual rankings. Agree credit, access, and time expectations.
- Treat qualitative feedback and a small voluntary sample as design evidence.
  Report selection, task type and skill differences. Do not generalize a small
  pilot to field-wide retention, productivity or neglectedness.
- Keep a supported correction or failed reproduction eligible as a useful
  output. A passing completion checklist does not validate a scientific claim.

## Relationship to Existing Ideas

FID-045 supplies source-review tasks and FID-106 supplies reproducibility
tasks. This call compares what makes those tasks usable for contributors and
reviewers. It works through the existing contribution process.

## Outputs

- Two starter tasks and a short artifact-quality rubric.
- A record of access and review bottlenecks and the cost of addressing them.
- Evidence on which task-packet elements to keep, revise, or retire.

## Open Questions

- Which barriers come from task wording, access, background knowledge, or reviewer capacity?
- Which parts of a worked example help contributors without giving away the answer?
- Does the packet save enough review time to justify keeping its inputs current?

## Ways to Help

- Dry-run one public first task for 30 minutes using the research-task template.
  Return what you produced, the first blocker if any, and one change an
  independent reviewer can check.
- Review a submitted source note or reproduction record against the stated completion criteria.

## Public Claim Boundary

This proposal concerns a bounded study of research tasks. It does not establish
a hiring, admissions, compensation, affiliation, or mentorship offer.
