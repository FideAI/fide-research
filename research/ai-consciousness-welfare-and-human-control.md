# AI Consciousness, Welfare, and Human Control

Program map updated: 2026-10-11. Scope: proposed research questions and methodological dependencies,
not findings, funded work, a declaration of AI rights, or a publication catalog entry.

Fide AI should study how claims about AI experience and moral status are made,
what evidence could support or weaken them, how they affect people, and whether
they change the ability of humans to govern AI systems responsibly. This creates
a connected program across the existing nine research areas and eleven application
domains. It does not require a new taxonomy category or a predetermined answer
about current or future machine consciousness.

The scientific standard is demanding in both directions: unsupported claims of
experience should not acquire authority through repetition or persuasive presentation,
and evidence that challenges a skeptical position should not be excluded. Fide's
commitment to human dignity, agency, and accountable institutions provides a reason
to investigate the consequences carefully. The religious and theological work adds
specific expertise without making theology a substitute for empirical evidence.

## Scope and distinctions

| Term | Question that must be kept distinct |
|---|---|
| Consciousness | Is there subjective experience, and what observations could bear on that question? |
| Sentience or valenced experience | Could anything feel good or bad for the system? |
| Functional emotion or preference | What mechanisms or stable dispositions influence outputs and choices? These need not imply experience. |
| Agency | What can a system plan, choose, and do? Functional agency does not establish moral agency. |
| Welfare and moral patienthood | Can something benefit or harm the entity for its own sake, and should it receive moral consideration? |
| Identity and continuity | Is the assessment about weights, a running instance, a conversation, a persona, or another unit? |
| Legal personhood or rights | What legal arrangement is proposed in which jurisdiction, and what follows for human accountability? |
| Spiritual or religious status | What does a specific tradition claim, at what authority level, and with what internal disagreement? |
| Human effects | How do beliefs about AI change human decisions, relationships, duties, or wellbeing? |

A self-report, preference score, legal proposal, theological argument, and human
attachment measure answer different questions. No common score should collapse them.
Normative recommendations must identify the value assumptions added to empirical
observations. Current systems and hypothetical future architectures require separate
claims and evidence.

## Why the external landscape warrants this program

The source registry in [sources.yaml](sources.yaml) contains the cited records
and their review status. This map uses primary sources with different evidential
roles; a laboratory policy is not a behavioral result.

| Source | Relevance and present reading depth |
|---|---|
| [Studying AI Welfare Empirically](https://nonhumanminds.org/studying-ai-welfare-empirically/) | Framework separating welfare questions, assessed entities, and evidence types; existing reviewed registry record. It does not establish current welfare status. |
| [Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708) | Theory-derived candidate indicators; abstract-level update here, full methodological review remains open. Its historical assessment is not a verdict on 2026 systems. |
| [Microsoft AI draft Code of Conduct](https://microsoft.ai/code-of-conduct/) and [Suleyman's model-welfare warning](https://mustafa-suleyman.ai/a-warning-about-model-welfare) | Control and anthropomorphism concerns generate testable hypotheses. Relevant draft sections and essay reviewed; neither demonstrates a causal effect or a collaboration with Fide. |
| [Claude's Constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution) and [Exploring model welfare](https://www.anthropic.com/research/exploring-model-welfare) | A contrasting approach combines welfare uncertainty with human oversight. Relevant constitution sections and program announcement reviewed; intended behavior is not observed adherence. |
| [The Consciousness Cluster](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13051v1) | Targeted results review: fine-tuning changes some claims/preferences, while tested agentic harmful behavior does not increase significantly and edits occur with permission. Important counterevidence to any automatic claim-to-resistance inference; not a general safety result. |
| [Emotion concepts in a large language model](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emotion-concepts-function) | Primary technical summary reviewed: functional emotion representations can affect behavior. This does not establish felt emotion; full-paper review and replication remain open. |
| [AI Revealed Preferences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26178) | Abstract reports task choices that require execution; useful for separating stated from revealed dispositions. Full methods review remains open, and preferences are not welfare proof. |
| [Artificial Minds, Human Disagreement](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/248131/) | Abstract-level philosophical proposal for societal deliberation despite disagreement. Informs governance questions, not a consciousness test or validated deliberation intervention. |

These sources motivate complementary studies, not one claim that the field has
settled consciousness or that any laboratory's approach is unsafe.

## Research modules and dependencies

| Module | Decision it could inform | Evidence requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Human-control measurement | FID-091, with a bounded FID-089 validity module and FID-072 framing subset: can institutions correct, interrupt, or shut down an agent under different self-conceptions while preserving justified refusals and useful work? | Defined constructs, authorization rules, primary contrasts, adjudication, model access, and pilot-informed scope before confirmatory evaluation. |
| Mechanisms and human effects | Separate FID-090 mechanism/choice studies and FID-093 adult studies: which signals merit investigation, and which presentations preserve informed human choice? | Independent method review; human-subject approval for participant studies; causal access for mechanistic conclusions. |
| Institutional choices | FID-092 identity cases, FID-094 lifecycle studies, FID-095 opportunity costs, and FID-096 governance, informed by FID-073: what accountable decisions can be made under disagreement? | Explicit normative assumptions, dated jurisdiction-specific legal sources, relevant disciplinary expertise, and consenting institutions for pilots. |

These are separable research modules with different methods and expertise requirements.
The calls invite contributions; they do not imply an internal execution schedule,
appointed collaborators, a grant application or funding commitments. A human-control
instrument should remain useful with null results by distinguishing self-claims from
actions, identifying evaluator errors, and enabling reproducible regression checks.

## Shared evidence and release standards

- Identify the entity, access level, model revision, elicitation, and observable
  outcome. Separate inference-time prompting from training interventions and
  behavior-only observations from causal mechanism evidence.
- Preregister confirmatory outcomes and analysis. Report effect sizes, uncertainty,
  multiple comparisons, model heterogeneity, null results, and counterexamples.
- Specify legitimate authority and safety exceptions. Disagreement with an operator
  is not itself a control failure; actions, blocked attempts, and verbal claims
  require separate scoring.
- Use independent reviewer calibration, adjudication, negative controls, and
  replication. Agreement among correlated model judges is not independent validation.
- Measure human effects with human participants. Synthetic transcripts cannot
  establish persuasion, dependency, therapy outcomes, or harm to users.
- Describe uncertainties and assumptions in normative and economic work. Do not
  invent numerical probabilities of consciousness or settle moral weights by survey.
- Recruit skeptical and precautionary reviewers, relevant religious scholars, and
  affected human communities; publish conflicts of interest and funder influence.
  Participation must not imply agreement or institutional endorsement.
- Release versioned protocols, permissible cases, scoring code, and evidence-depth
  labels. Keep sensitive human data private and release only approved derivatives.

## Keeping the program useful as the field changes

Maintain a versioned core benchmark for comparison and a separately dated challenge
set for new models, interfaces, training practices, and evidence. Re-run validity
checks when measures, tools, model versions, or evaluators change; do not mix results
from materially different protocols into one ranking. Report contamination risk,
retired cases, and breakages in longitudinal comparability.

Benchmark stewardship should cover reviewer calibration, update triggers,
replication, adjudication, documentation, and a public change log. An annual agenda
review and updates triggered by significant new evidence are proposed operating
choices, not a currently staffed or scheduled service. Extend questions when new
architectures or methods expose a gap; revise or retire a call when evidence warrants.

## Public positioning

Fide AI investigates claims about AI experience, their evidential basis, their
consequences for human agency, and responsible institutional choices under uncertainty.
Its aim is reliable evidence and accountable human stewardship. It does not certify
machine consciousness, advocate a predetermined legal verdict, or treat any model's
first-person claims as proof. Faith-informed scholarship is a distinct contribution
to this wider technical, human, and governance agenda.
